# N4 EDA — Key Findings

* The overall late-delivery rate was approximately **7.84%** (5,291 late orders out of 67,533).
* Actual delivery time showed a clear difference between the two classes: late orders averaged **36.22 days**, compared with **11.92 days** for on-time orders.
* Estimated delivery time was very similar between the two groups, averaging **24.08 days for late orders** and **24.64 days for on-time orders**.
* Customer-seller distance showed a clear positive relationship with late deliveries. The late rate increased from **3.79%** for orders within 0–100 km to **13.66%** for orders traveling more than 2,000 km.
* Purchase month showed noticeable variation in late-delivery rates, with higher rates observed in some later months of the dataset. Months with very few orders were interpreted cautiously.
* Numerical correlations with the delivery label were generally weak, suggesting that simple linear correlation does not fully capture the factors associated with delivery delays.

## Conclusion

Geographical distance and actual delivery duration showed the clearest differences between late and on-time orders. Temporal variables may also provide useful information, while individual order-value and payment-related variables showed relatively weak linear relationships with delivery delays.
